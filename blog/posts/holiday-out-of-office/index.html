Take a Real Holiday: An AI Routine for Your Out-of-Office

Work-Life Balance Out-of-Office Automation

A notary in Lausanne books a week in the mountains and then quietly cancels it. Every time she thinks about leaving, the same picture appears: an inbox that fills up, clients who need an answer, and a mountain of open pendences waiting on her desk when she returns. So the holiday keeps getting postponed.

Time off is one of the hardest things to take in a small business. There is no one to hand the work to, and the fear of the re-entry keeps you glued to the laptop. This article describes a simple routine for actually disconnecting: the AI prepares your out-of-office, drafts a handover for the few things that truly cannot wait, and tidies up before you come back.

Why time off keeps getting skipped

Most self-employed people and small offices do not have a stand-in. The reasons for staying are usually the same:

  • The re-entry looks scary: the inbox, the missed calls and the open pendences all pile up while you are away.
  • There is no one to hand over to: the business runs on your head, so leaving feels impossible.
  • The out-of-office is hard to write: a good one does more than say "I am away" — and that takes time you do not have before departure.

The result is predictable: you never really switch off, the holiday is half-work anyway, and you come back more tired than you left.

A three-step routine

Step 1 — Write the out-of-office before you leave

A week before departure, give the AI your travel dates, your usual tone and one or two contact people. It drafts an out-of-office reply that is warm, clear and genuinely useful — it states when you are back, who can help in the meantime, and what a client should do if it is urgent.

I am away from 24 to 31 August. Write a short out-of-office reply in my usual tone. State the dates, point clients to my colleague Anna for urgent matters, and add one friendly sentence telling them I will answer everything else when I am back. No drama, just a clear, warm note.

Step 2 — A quiet handover for the few things that cannot wait

Before you leave, make a short list of what is open. The AI turns it into a tidy handover note: what each item is about, where it stands, and what the next step is. You send it to your stand-in, a colleague or your fiduciary — and you genuinely let go.

Here are my open pendences for the week I am away. Sort them into two groups: things that can wait until I am back, and things someone must handle now. For each urgent item, write one line on what it is about and what the next step is.

Step 3 — Re-enter without the pile-up

On the day you return, the AI summarises what happened while you were away: the new emails, the answered ones, and anything that still needs you. You start with a tidy list instead of a wall of unread messages.

Summarise my inbox from the last week. List the new clients, the questions that were already answered, and the few things that still need my attention. Keep it to one page.

Why this works in practice

  • It removes the fear of leaving: the out-of-office and the handover already exist, so there is nothing left to dread.
  • It makes the re-entry: you come back to a summary, not a pile.
  • It protects the relationship: clients get a warm, clear answer even while you are away.

What this looks like across professions

  • Self-employed and independent professionals: a proper out-of-office and a quiet handover, so the holiday actually happens.
  • Fiduciaries: a clear note for clients about who handles what during the absence, always in the same tone.
  • Doctors and small practices: a locum handover that covers the open patients and the pending results.
  • Notaries and small offices: a tidy list of what is open, so the stand-in steps in without guesswork.

The routine is the same everywhere; only the wording changes.

A note on tone and data

The tone matters as much as the words. A good out-of-office is warm and short: state the dates, name the person who helps, and keep the promise to answer the rest on return. On data, keep the same rule as everywhere else: no confidential details in a public chatbot. Client names and file numbers stay out of the prompt; anonymise first and add the real names back into the final note yourself.

The outcome

One quiet hour before departure is usually enough to prepare a real holiday. The out-of-office is written, the handover is clear, and the re-entry is a single page instead of a pile. You switch off, come back rested, and the clients never noticed you were gone — because a prepared absence looks a lot like a well-run business.
