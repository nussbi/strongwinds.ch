Meta's New AI Agent for Small Businesses: Worth a Try?

AI AgentsAutomationData Privacy

Meta just pushed its AI assistant, Muse, into the small-business world. It plugs into the tools you already use — Slack, Zoom, Canva, QuickBooks, Shopify, Stripe and more — and promises to save you hours. Here is what it actually does, and whether a Swiss SME should care.

What Muse for Small Business is

Muse is Meta’s AI agent, and the small-business version connects it to the software you already run your company with. Meta lists integrations with Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Intuit QuickBooks, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, Zoom and others. The pitch is that, because Muse can see your tools, it has the context of your whole operation — what you sell, how your brand sounds, and the questions customers ask most — instead of being a blank chatbot that knows nothing about you.

The service is free with usage limits, and you pay for a subscription if you want more. Meta is clear about the target: owners who are short on hours, not on ideas.

What it can actually do for you

In practice, an agent like this is useful for the repetitive, low-stakes work that eats your evenings. Drafting replies, summarising a thread, turning a note into a to-do, finding a document, or writing a first version of an update to a customer. Because it sits inside the tools you already use, you do not have to copy and paste between a chatbot and your real work.

The honest limit is the same as with every agent: it is strong on drafting and summarising, and weaker on anything that needs your judgement. Let it write the first version; you make the call.

The part worth pausing over: your data

Muse is a cloud service run by Meta. Anything you let it read — customer names, order history, messages, financials — is processed on Meta’s infrastructure. That matters for a Swiss business in two ways. First, customer data has to stay protected under Swiss and European data-protection rules. Second, you are handing a very large company a detailed picture of how you do business.

None of this means you cannot use it. It means you should be deliberate: start with non-sensitive tasks, do not connect your accounting or customer files on day one, and treat anything you type as if it were going into a shared system.

Should you try it?

If you are curious, yes — start small. Pick one repetitive task, connect one tool, and run it for a week. Compare the time saved against the time spent checking its work. If it earns its place, keep it; if not, you have lost nothing.

The bigger picture is more interesting than any single tool: the big platforms are now betting that small businesses want an agent that knows their whole operation. Whether you use Meta’s version or a local one you run yourself, the useful habit is the same — hand the routine to a machine, and keep the judgement for yourself.
