One AI for the Whole Office: A Private Assistant for Every Team Member

Sales CRM Admin

Most AI tools are built for one person with one login. But a small office is not one person — it is two, three or five people who all need a hand with drafting, summarising and searching. A new open-source tool called Octop turns one ordinary computer into an AI assistant for the whole team: every colleague gets their own, and all the data stays in your own office.

What it actually is

Octop is a small program you install once, on a computer or small server that stays switched on in the office. Instead of paying a monthly fee for every employee to use an AI, you run one assistant for everyone.

Each person on your team gets their own AI “agent” — a helper with its own name and personality that remembers that person’s own notes and tasks. You can also share a common knowledge base, so the assistant that drafts a letter for one colleague already knows how your office talks to clients.

People reach the assistant through a simple web page, or directly in Telegram — so there is nothing new to install on anyone’s phone or laptop, and no separate logins to manage.

Why that matters for a small Swiss office

Three things make this interesting for a self-employed practice or a small firm. First, the price: one computer, one setup, no per-seat subscription that grows with every new hire.

  • Client data stays with you. Everything runs on your own machine, so sensitive names, medical notes or financial details never travel to a foreign cloud. That sits comfortably with the Swiss Data Protection Act (DSG).
  • Everyone gets their own assistant. The receptionist drafts replies, the accountant summarises documents, the boss asks questions — each with a helper that knows their own corner of the work.
  • One shared “office brain.” Notes, templates and how-you-do-things live in one place, so the whole team speaks with one voice.

The honest caveats

This is still young software, and it is not for everyone today. You need someone who is comfortable with the command line to set it up, and a computer that stays switched on. It is built by Tencent Cloud, so if you are cautious, read the code before you trust it with client data. And because everything runs in one process, if that process stops, the assistant stops too — it is not a bank-grade system yet.

None of this makes it bad. It makes it early. Treat it as a promising direction, not a finished product.

Is it worth a try?

If you work alone, start simpler: a single local assistant is easier to manage, and we have written about that before. If you have a team of three to five people and someone technical in the house, Octop is a real, low-cost way to give everyone a private AI without sending client data to the cloud.

Our advice is the same as always: pick one small, repetitive task — drafting a standard email, summarising a document — and let the team’s assistant do that first. If it saves real time, add a second task. That is how a useful tool earns its place.
