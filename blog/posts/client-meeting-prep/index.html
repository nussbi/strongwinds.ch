The One-Page Client Brief: AI Prep Before Every Meeting

PreparationClientsWorkflow

For a notary, a fiduciary, a doctor's practice or any small office, the client meeting is where the real work happens. Yet most of us walk into it half-prepared: the notes are scattered across emails, a notebook and a few post-its, and the open points from last time have quietly faded from memory.

This article describes a preparation routine. Ten minutes before an important meeting, the AI gathers everything you know about the client into a single page. You read it, correct it once, and walk in knowing exactly where you left off. No new software project — just a short, repeatable prompt and a little discipline.

Why meetings start cold

It is rarely laziness. It is the natural result of how small offices store information.

  • Notes live in many places: part in the inbox, part in a paper notebook, part in the CRM, part in your head.
  • The last meeting fades: decisions that felt obvious in March are vague in July.
  • Open points slip away: the promise to send a document, the follow-up on an offer, the half-finished answer.
  • Nobody likes being asked twice: a client who has to repeat their situation feels unheard, and trust quietly erodes.

What a one-page brief looks like

The brief does not need to be clever. It needs to be complete. Five short sections are usually enough:

  • Who: the client, the key people on their side, and what matters to them.
  • Context: what was decided last time, and what has happened since.
  • Open points: every deadline and promise that is still outstanding — on both sides.
  • Goal: what this meeting should actually achieve.
  • Questions: the things you want to confirm or clarify.

A three-step routine

The routine fits into ten quiet minutes before a meeting, and the AI does the mechanical work. You stay in charge of every judgement call.

Step 1 — Gather the raw material

Collect the last few emails, your notes and the previous brief into one place. You do not need to tidy them first; the AI handles the mess.

Here are my notes and emails from my last contacts with this client. Create a one-page brief with these sections: who they are, context, open points, goal for the next meeting, and questions to ask. Keep it factual, do not invent anything, and mark anything unclear with a question mark.

Step 2 — Correct, don't rewrite

The first draft is rarely perfect, but it is always a head start. You correct names, trim a point that is no longer relevant, and add the one thing the AI could not know. Five minutes of editing replaces an hour of remembering.

Step 3 — Walk in and follow up on the open points

After the meeting, update the brief while it is still fresh. Add the new decisions and deadlines, and move the closed points to a short "done" note. The next brief builds on this one, so preparation gets faster every time.

Here is my meeting brief and my notes from today's meeting. Update the brief: add the new decisions and deadlines, mark the resolved points as done, and list the open points that remain for next time.

What the AI does not do

The brief is your responsibility, not the AI's. It never decides what is important, never invents a fact, and never judges a client. It only organises what you already know into a readable page. If a detail is missing, it should say so rather than guess.

Client briefs contain names, numbers and sometimes health or legal context, so the usual data-protection rules apply. Use a business-grade tool rather than a private consumer account, check whether the provider stores your input or trains on it, and anonymise first if you are unsure — build the brief with placeholders and add the real names at the end.

The outcome

Ten minutes of preparation turns a vague, half-remembered conversation into a focused meeting where the client feels genuinely remembered. Open points stop slipping away, follow-ups happen on time, and the relationship — the real asset of every small office — quietly gets stronger.

Start with your next important meeting. One page, three steps, and you will never walk in cold again.
