From First Contact to First Invoice: A Client-Intake Workflow with AI

IntakeWorkflowClients

Every new client starts the same way: an enquiry, a handful of exchanged messages, a first meeting, and then the real work. For notaries, fiduciaries, doctors and small offices, the steps between “hello” and a signed mandate are where time quietly disappears — and where details get dropped.

This article describes a practical intake workflow. It works for anyone who welcomes new clients in Switzerland: the fiduciary opening a new dossier, the notary preparing a first appointment, the doctor registering a new patient. No software project, no big setup. Just a simple routine that keeps you organised from the first message to the first invoice.

Why client intake gets messy

Intake sounds simple, but in practice it is a small project with many moving parts. The client’s details arrive scattered across emails, phone calls and handwritten notes. Documents come in one at a time. And because there is no single place where everything lives, small things slip: a missing ID scan, an unanswered question, a promise that “we’ll sort that out later”.

The cost of a messy intake is not dramatic, which is exactly why it persists. But it adds up:

  • Double work: the same information is collected twice because nobody remembered the first time.
  • Slow starts: the first meeting arrives and you are still hunting for the documents you asked for two weeks ago.
  • A poor first impression: the client asked a question on Monday and heard back on Thursday, without a clear “here is what happens next”.

A intake in four steps

The workflow has four steps. You can do them in one sitting when a new enquiry arrives, or spread them across the first week. The order matters less than the habit of writing everything down once.

Step 1: One intake summary

When an enquiry arrives, let the AI turn the scattered messages into a single client profile. The goal is not to automate your judgement — it is to get every fact onto one page before you decide anything.

Here is a new client enquiry (emails and notes below). Create a short profile: name and contact details, what they are asking for, what has already been agreed, and which questions are still open. Do not invent any detail — use a placeholder [..] where something is missing. Enquiry: [paste the messages]

Keep this profile in the client’s folder. It becomes the single source of truth for the whole intake, and it takes the pressure off your memory.

Step 2: The document checklist

Every practice has a standard set of documents it needs: ID, proof of address, the relevant forms, the previous year’s documents. Let the AI draft that list for this specific client, so nothing is forgotten.

Based on the client profile, write a short checklist of the documents I still need from this client. Group them by priority and note which ones are legally required. Keep it plain and friendly, so I can send it to the client as it is.

Review the list once against your own experience, then send it. A clear checklist is the single most effective way to prevent the “we forgot the form” phone call.

Step 3: A welcome message that explains the next steps

After the first contact, the client wants to know what happens next. Let the AI draft a short, welcome message that confirms the facts and states the next step — with no pressure, no jargon, and one clear question for the client to answer.

Draft a short welcome email for this client. Confirm what we discussed, list the documents they should send, and state the next step in one sentence. Tone: friendly and factual, no urgency. Do not invent dates or details.

Read the draft once, adjust it to your voice, and send it. The client gets clarity, and you get a paper trail.

Step 4: Prepare the first meeting

Before the first appointment, let the AI turn the client profile into a short agenda: what to confirm, what to explain, and which questions to ask. This is the same preparation you do mentally — just written down and complete.

Using the client profile, write a short agenda for our first meeting: the points to confirm, the points to explain, and three questions I should ask. Keep it to one page. Do not add anything that is not in the profile.

Arriving with an agenda changes the meeting. You spend less time searching for context and more time listening — which is the part the client actually values.

What stays human

AI can summarise, list and draft, but it cannot take responsibility for a client relationship. The decision about whether to take on a mandate, what to charge, and how to handle a sensitive matter stays with you. The workflow simply removes the mechanical parts — the copying, the listing, the remembering — so that your attention goes where it belongs.

Data protection: client details are not neutral

Client intake is full of personal data — names, addresses, sometimes health or financial details. Once you paste it into an AI tool, the Swiss Data Protection Act (DSG) applies. In practice, three habits keep it simple:

  • Use a business-grade tool, not a private consumer account.
  • Check the small print: does the provider store your input or train on it?
  • Anonymise first if you are unsure: replace names with placeholders, draft the structure, and add the real details afterwards.

Start with one new client. Run the enquiry through the first step, write the checklist, and see how much lighter the first week feels. The whole point of an intake is simple: the client is welcomed properly, and you keep your week.
