Saying the Hard Things Calmly: An AI Routine for Difficult Client Conversations

ClientsCorrespondenceQuality

The difficult conversations are the ones that follow you home. Raising a price, saying no to a request, admitting a mistake, telling a client their deadline will slip — these are the moments that keep a notary, a fiduciary, a doctor or a small office owner awake at night. Not because the work itself is hard, but because the words feel hard to find.

You cannot delegate the conversation itself. It has to come from you, in your voice, with your judgement. But you can prepare for it the way you prepare for anything else that matters: calmly, in advance, with a clear head. AI is not there to speak for you. It is there to help you think it through, find the right words, and rehearse the parts that usually trip you up.

Why difficult conversations feel so heavy

The resistance is rarely about the content. It is about what the conversation might cost. A few of the usual reasons:

  • Fear of losing the client. You want to say no, but you worry the relationship will end on the spot.
  • Wanting to be liked. For many small practices, the client is also a neighbour, a friend, or a long-standing acquaintance.
  • Not finding the words. You know roughly what to say, but not how to open, and so you keep postponing.
  • Making it bigger than it is. The longer it sits, the larger it grows, until a small correction feels like a confrontation.

A calm routine, in four steps

You do not need a script. You need about fifteen quiet minutes, once, before the conversation happens.

Step 1 — Put the facts on the table

Write down the bare facts, without any interpretation: what is changing, by how much, from when, and why. For a price increase, the new rate and the date it takes effect. For a no, exactly which request you are declining. For bad news, what happened and what you are doing about it. Facts first, feelings second.

Step 2 — Let the AI draft the opening lines

The first two sentences are the hardest, so let the AI propose a few. Give it the facts, your tone, and what you want to avoid:

I need to tell a client that my hourly rate will rise from CHF 180 to CHF 200 from 1 January. Write three short, calm openings for the message. Plain language, no hedging, no long justification, no apology for existing. Keep each to two or three sentences. Sound like a professional, not a corporation.

Step 3 — Ask the AI to play the client

This is the part most people skip, and the part that makes the difference. Ask the AI to take the client's side and push back, so you can prepare your answers in private:

Play the client for a moment. You are a long-standing client who feels the increase is too high and wonders why it is happening now. Raise the two or three most likely objections, one at a time. For each, I will answer, and you should reply realistically. Keep it calm and businesslike.

Step 4 — Read it aloud once

Take the draft that feels most like you, and read it aloud once. If a sentence sounds like something you would never say, change it. The goal is not a perfect script — it is a version of the conversation that is already yours before it starts.

Three conversations that keep coming back

The same three situations repeat in almost every practice. Each gets easier with the routine above:

  • Raising your prices. State the new rate, the date, and one short reason. Then stop talking and let the client react.
  • Saying no. Decline the request clearly, and if you can, offer one alternative. A clean no with a direction is kinder than a vague maybe.
  • Delivering bad news. A missed deadline, an error, an unexpected cost. Say what happened, what you are doing about it, and what happens next — in that order.

What AI cannot do for you

The AI helps you prepare; it does not deliver the message for you, and it should not. The relationship, the tone, the honesty — those are yours, and they are the whole point. One more rule that should go without saying: do not paste client names, patient details or confidential figures into a public model. Use a local model, or anonymise the facts before you ask for help with the words.

The outcome

After one or two prepared conversations, you will notice the difference. The knot in your stomach is smaller, because you have already been through the difficult part once — quietly, on your own, with the AI as a sparring partner. The conversation itself becomes what it always should have been: a short, honest exchange between two people who respect each other. That is the whole point of the routine. Not to win, and not to soften the message into nothing. Simply to say what needs saying, in Ruhe.
