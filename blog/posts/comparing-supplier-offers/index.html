Comparing Offers Without the Guesswork: An AI Routine for Choosing the Right Supplier

OffersCostsPlanning

Choosing a supplier should be a simple decision. Yet every small Swiss office knows the feeling: four offers on the desk, each formatted differently, each hiding its real cost somewhere in the fine print. Insurance, phone, IT support, practice software — the numbers never line up, so you end up guessing.

Whether it is a new liability insurance for a notary, a phone plan for a two-person fiduciary office, or practice software for a medical practice, the moment of choice arrives with the same quiet pressure: you have to pick one, and you would rather not be wrong.

Most people compare offers by reading them one after the other and trusting a vague feeling. That works until a hidden fee, a shorter guarantee or a two-year minimum term quietly changes the picture. The decision feels hard because the offers were never put side by side.

Why comparing "by eye" quietly fails

The eye is good at many things, but not at lining up numbers that are deliberately spread across different formats:

  • Hidden costs. Setup fees, per-seat licences, cancellation terms buried deep in the annex.
  • Different units. One quote is per month, another per year, a third per user — you cannot compare them directly.
  • Recency bias. The last offer you read feels the clearest, even when it is not the best.
  • Forgotten questions. The missing detail only surfaces after you have already signed.

A calm routine for comparing offers

You do not need a spreadsheet expert or a procurement department. You need a small, repeatable routine that brings every offer into the same shape before you decide.

Step 1 — Write down what you actually need

Before you read a single offer, list your own requirements on one page: what the service must do, how many people will use it, and what you are not willing to pay for. This is your reference list. It keeps you from being talked into extras you will never use.

Step 2 — Collect every offer in one place

Save all quotes as PDF or text into a single folder, one file per supplier. Add the sales emails and any annexes. Name each file simply: supplier and date. If a supplier only quoted you verbally, ask for it in writing — an offer you cannot re-read is not comparable.

Step 3 — Let the AI build one comparison table

Feed the offers to the AI and ask it to line them up. Here is the prompt:

Here are three offers for [service, e.g. business insurance / practice software]. Turn them into a single comparison table. Rows: total cost per year, setup fee, minimum term, cancellation notice, what is included, what is excluded, and any hidden fees. Use plain language. Mark anything that differs strongly between suppliers or looks unusual.

Step 4 — Ask the questions that actually matter

Once the table is there, read it and send the AI your follow-up questions. The second prompt turns the table into a decision aid:

Based on the table above, which offer is the best value for a [type of practice] with [size / number of people]? What am I paying for in each offer that I may not need? What should I ask each supplier before I sign?

Run the whole routine in one sitting, in a single quiet hour. Once you have done it twice, it becomes a habit: every renewal notice, a price increase, or a competitor's quote lands in the same folder and gets compared the same calm way — no more deciding under pressure in the moment.

What AI cannot decide for you

The AI can line up the numbers, but the decision stays yours. It will not know your relationship with a supplier, how reliable their support actually is, or which features you will genuinely use in a year. And it can misread a complex pricing model — so treat the table as a strong first draft and check the two or three figures that matter most yourself.

The outcome

What used to be an afternoon of frustrated re-reading becomes thirty calm minutes. You get one clear table, a short list of questions to ask, and a decision you can explain to yourself — and to anyone who asks why you chose that supplier. No more signing the offer you happened to read last.
