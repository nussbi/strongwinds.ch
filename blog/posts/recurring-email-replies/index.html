Replies That Sound Like You: An AI Routine for Your Recurring Emails

Email Templates Clients

A notary in Zurich writes the same three sentences about the next appointment a dozen times a week. A dentist in Geneva confirms, reschedules and sends the same payment details every day. A fiduciary in Bern answers "please find attached" so often it has become muscle memory. None of it is difficult — it simply never stops, and every reply starts from a blank page.

These recurring emails are the quiet background hum of a small practice. They are not billable, not strategic and rarely remembered — but they still have to be written well, because they are the voice your clients actually hear most often.

Why recurring emails quietly drain you

The cost is not one big problem. It is a hundred tiny ones, repeated every single day:

  • Starting from scratch. You rewrite the same appointment confirmation from memory, slightly differently each time.
  • An uneven tone. The reply you write at seven in the morning sounds different from the one you write at five in the afternoon, tired, after a long day.
  • Small errors. By the third repetition of the day the typos creep in, the wrong address gets pasted, the attachment is forgotten.

The result is familiar: replies that sound rushed, vary from day to day, and quietly steal the time you meant to spend on real work.

A simple routine in three steps

You do not need new software or a complicated system. You need one unhurried hour to build a small library of replies that already sound like you.

Step 1 — Collect your recurring emails

For one week, notice which emails you write more than once: appointment confirmations, requests for missing documents, payment details, directions to the office, the short "thank you", the gentle reminder. Write the list down. Most practices have between five and fifteen of these.

Step 2 — Let the AI draft templates in your voice

Hand the AI a few of your own real replies — with names and sensitive details removed — and ask it to turn them into reusable templates that match your tone. A prompt like this does the heavy lifting:

I run a small practice in Switzerland. Below are a few real emails I have written to clients. Please draft reusable templates in my tone for the messages I write most often: an appointment confirmation, a request for missing documents, a note with the payment details, and a short thank-you. Keep the wording natural and friendly, leave placeholders like [name], [date] and [amount] where something changes, and do not add any marketing phrases I did not use. Emails: [paste your own, names removed].

Step 3 — Save, review, reuse

Store the finished templates where you can reach them in one click — in your email program's template or signature feature, in a simple notes file, or in a small document. When a recurring email comes up, open the template, fill in the one or two lines that change, read it once and send.

A second prompt for the tricky ones

Some recurring messages need a little more care — a rescheduling note, a small price change, a delay. For those, ask the AI to keep the tone warm and to say no pressure:

Draft a short, friendly message to a client to reschedule next week's appointment. Tone: warm, no urgency. Mention that a new time can be found together and that there is no rush. Leave a placeholder for the proposed new date.

What the AI does well — and what stays with you

  • Drafting the structure: the same complete, friendly skeleton for every message, so nothing is forgotten.
  • Matching your voice: the AI learns your wording from your own examples, so replies stop sounding generic.
  • Keeping it consistent: every client gets the same professional tone, no matter when you write.

What stays with you is the human part: the personal touch, the judgement about what a particular client needs to hear, and the final approval before anything is sent.

A note on data and privacy

Templates are usually harmless — a standard appointment confirmation carries no personal data. But the moment a reply contains a client's name, health details or financial figures, do not paste it into a public chatbot. Use a local model or a zero-retention service, or anonymise the message first and add the real names back yourself. The Swiss Data Protection Act applies to client data even in something as small as an email.

The outcome

One hour at the start builds a small library that saves you ten minutes every day. Replies go out faster, they sound consistently like you, and the small errors disappear. You stop re-typing the same sentence for the hundredth time — and keep the energy for the work that actually needs you.
