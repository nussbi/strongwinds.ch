Know Where Your Business Stands Every Month

Monthly reviewBusiness overviewReporting

Running a business and understanding it are two different jobs. Most self-employed people have their numbers somewhere — in a bank account, an invoice folder, a bookkeeping tool — but rarely in one place where the whole picture is visible. This routine gives you a short monthly summary of where your business actually stands.

Gather the numbers once a month

Pick a fixed day — the first Monday, the last Friday, whichever suits you — and spend fifteen minutes pulling together the basics: money in, money out, invoices sent, invoices still open, and any large expenses.

You do not need perfect bookkeeping for this. Rough figures are enough to see the direction of travel. If the numbers live in a tool, export them; if they live in your head, write them down.

Hand these figures to an AI tool and ask for a plain-language summary: one page, no jargon, three or four key numbers and what they mean.

Keep a simple template so the process is the same every month. The less you have to think about how to gather the numbers, the more likely you are to actually do it.

Ask the right questions

The quality of the summary depends on the questions you ask. Instead of “summarise my month”, try: what came in, what went out, what is still owed to me, and what surprised you about this month.

Ask the AI to compare with the previous month, so you can see whether things are moving up, down, or staying flat. Trends matter more than single numbers.

Keep the summary short. If it runs longer than a page, it is no longer a summary — it is a report, and you will not read it.

Turn the summary into one decision

A summary is only useful if it changes something. At the end of each monthly read, pick one small action: chase one overdue invoice, cancel one unused expense, or set one goal for next month.

Write that single action at the top of the summary, so it is the first thing you see next time. Over a year, twelve small actions add up to a very different business.

Do not let the summary grow a life of its own. One action per month is enough. If you try to fix everything at once, nothing gets fixed.

  1. Pull the numbers.
  2. Ask for a one-page summary.
  3. Note the trend versus last month.
  4. Choose one action and write it down.

What you gain

You stop guessing how the month went and start knowing. You catch problems early, while they are still small and cheap to fix. And you build a quiet habit of steering your business instead of just reacting to it.

This routine costs a quarter of an hour a month and gives back a clearer head the rest of the time. That is a good trade. After a few months, the trend lines start to tell a story of their own.
