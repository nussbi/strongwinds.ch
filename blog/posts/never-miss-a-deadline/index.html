Never Miss a Deadline: An AI Routine for Legal and Tax Dates

DeadlinesRemindersPlanning

A notary in Lucerne has a contract registration deadline next Tuesday. A fiduciary in Basel has the MWST quarter closing this week. A doctor in Geneva promised a patient a report by Friday. None of them will miss it on purpose — the danger is that the date quietly slides past, buried in an email, a letter or a sticky note.

This article describes a simple routine for keeping every legal, tax and renewal date on one list — and letting the AI handle the sorting, the reminders and the first draft of the follow-ups. You stay in control of every decision. Nothing is automated behind your back.

The idea is not another app to check. It is one list and one short weekly habit — supported by AI, but run by you.

Why deadlines get missed

It is rarely carelessness. It is usually structure. Dates arrive scattered across many places, and nothing brings them together:

  • No single list: deadlines live in emails, letters, contracts and a calendar that nobody fully trusts.
  • Set-and-forget: a date is noted once, then never resurfaces until it is too late to act.
  • Too late a warning: the reminder arrives the day before, when the preparation needed weeks.

For a notary, a missed Frist can mean liability. For a fiduciary, a missed MWST date means a penalty. For a doctor, a late report erodes trust. The cost is real — and it is almost always avoidable.

A three-step routine

Step 1 — Collect every date into one list

Once, gather every deadline you know about: legal Fristen, tax and MWST dates, contract renewals, permit expiries, report promises. Put them into a simple list — a note, a spreadsheet, a document. Each entry only needs a date, a short name and a lead time (how many days before the date you want to start).

Step 2 — Let the AI sort and schedule

Hand the list to the AI and ask it to turn it into a forward schedule. The AI reads the lead times and works backwards: this report must start today, this renewal can wait until next month.

Here is my list of deadlines with dates and lead times. Sort them by the date I need to start acting, not by the deadline itself. For each, tell me the earliest sensible start date and what the first step is. Keep it clear and simple — no urgency, no « sofort ».

Step 3 — The weekly ten-minute review

Pick one fixed slot each week — ten minutes is enough. You review the coming two weeks, move anything that has changed, and let the AI draft the reminders and follow-ups you will actually send. This single habit is what turns a good list into a system that does not fail.

Let the AI draft the follow-up, not just the alarm

The useful part is not the reminder itself. It is the message that comes with it: the polite nudge to a client whose document is still missing, the note to a colleague, the internal checklist for a report. The AI drafts it; you approve it.

Draft a short, friendly reminder to my client in Swiss German (informal « du », no anglicisms). Their signed contract copy is still missing and I need it by Friday. Keep it friendly, one short paragraph, no pressure.

What this looks like for your profession

  • Notaries and law firms: registration and submission Fristen, contract milestones, notarisation dates.
  • Fiduciaries: MWST and tax deadlines, annual statements, payroll dates, client document deadlines.
  • Doctors and small offices: report promises, insurance and permit renewals, follow-up appointments.

The routine is the same everywhere. Only the names of the dates change.

A note on data

Deadline lists often contain client names, amounts and legal context. Keep the usual data-protection rules in mind: use a business-grade tool rather than a private consumer account, check whether the provider stores or trains on your input, and anonymise first if you are unsure — replace names with placeholders, prepare the schedule, then add the real names back at the end.

The outcome

One quiet hour to set it up, ten minutes a week to maintain it, and the number of dates that slip through drops to nearly zero. You stop carrying every deadline in your head, and your clients feel it — a professional who always shows up on time, without drama. Start with the next deadline that worries you.
