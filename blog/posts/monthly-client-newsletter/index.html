Stay Top of Mind with Clients: An AI Routine for Your Monthly Newsletter

NewsletterClientsMarketing

A fiduciary in Zug finishes a client's tax return and the relationship quietly goes quiet. A doctor in Lausanne treats a patient, and by the next quarter the patient has forgotten her name. A notary in Bern does excellent work, but when a past client needs legal help again, they cannot remember who to call. In every case the problem is the same: out of sight, out of mind.

For a small Swiss practice, the most valuable asset is trust built over years — but trust needs a gentle reminder now and then. A short monthly newsletter is the gentlest way to stay present in your clients' lives without selling, pushing or chasing. And with AI handling the mechanical parts, it takes half an hour a month, not a whole afternoon.

Why a newsletter works better than you think

Most self-employed people assume a newsletter means marketing, a mailing list and hours of writing. It does not have to. A quiet monthly note does three useful things:

  • It is permission-based and low-pressure. People signed up, so a monthly note feels welcome, not pushy.
  • It compounds quietly. One useful note a month, and after a year your clients think of you first when something comes up.
  • It is reusable. One topic can become an email, a LinkedIn post and a short page on your website.

A simple routine that takes 30 minutes

You do not need to become a writer or a marketer. You need a simple, repeatable routine that you can run once a month, in a quiet half hour, without stress.

Step 1 — Collect three small topics (5 minutes)

Note down what happened this month: a change in the law, a common client question, a small tip, or a deadline worth flagging. You do not need a big idea; useful beats clever every time.

Step 2 — Let the AI draft the newsletter

Give the AI your three topics and a couple of sentences about your practice. Ask for a short, warm newsletter in your voice.

Write a short monthly newsletter for my [notary / fiduciary / medical] practice in [language]. Tone: warm, plain, no marketing hype, no exclamation marks. Structure: a one-line greeting, three short paragraphs (one per topic below), and a closing line with a soft invitation to get in touch. Under 200 words. Topics: [paste your three topics].

Step 3 — Review, personalise, send

Read the draft once, add one personal detail (a seasonal greeting, a local note), check the names and dates, and send. Five minutes of review, and the whole thing is done.

Where to find topics without effort

The hardest part is usually deciding what to write about. Here is where the material hides:

  • Common client questions. The answer you give three times a week is a newsletter topic.
  • Legal and tax changes. Flag them before your clients ask — that is exactly when they trust you most.
  • Seasonal deadlines. Renewal dates, filing dates, check-up reminders. Useful, timely, easy to write.
  • A small note about your practice. A new team member, new opening hours, a short holiday notice.

Keep a simple running list. Whenever a client asks a good question or a deadline approaches, add a line to the list. At the end of the month, pick three.

What AI does well — and what stays with you

  • The blank page: the AI turns three bullet points into flowing, readable text.
  • Consistency: the same warm voice every month, even when you are busy.
  • The translations: if you serve clients in German, French and English, the AI can draft all three versions in minutes.

What stays with you is the judgement: which topics matter, what your clients actually need, and what you would never say. The AI drafts; you decide. That division never changes.

A note on data and privacy

A newsletter is generic, so there is usually nothing confidential in it. But keep individual client data — names, amounts, medical details — out of the draft. Describe the audience, not specific people. If you ever need to paste a client list or a case into a draft, use a local or zero-retention service instead of a public chatbot.

The outcome

Once a month, every client hears from you in a useful way. Referrals come more easily, past clients return, and you stop worrying about "marketing" because the newsletter does it quietly in the background. Thirty minutes a month, and you stay present without ever chasing anyone.
