Your Own AI Assistant on Your Own Computer: Client Data That Stays With You

Local AIData ProtectionAI Agents

Most AI assistants live far away, on a big company's servers. Every question you ask and every document you paste travels to that cloud and back. For a Swiss notary, doctor or fiduciary, that is a real concern: client data should not leave the office without a good reason. A small open-source tool called OpenClaw shows a different way — an assistant that runs on your own computer, where your data stays with you.

OpenClaw is what people call a “local AI gateway”. In plain words: it is a helper you install on your own machine, which lets an AI work with you — drafting, summarising, answering questions — while your files, your notes and your passwords never leave the building. No subscription, no cloud account, and no one else's server in between.

Why “local” matters for your practice

Under the revised Swiss Data Protection Act, you stay responsible for what happens to client data, even when a tool you installed misbehaves. Sending sensitive documents to a cloud assistant means trusting a third party with that data. Running the assistant locally is a cleaner answer: the data simply does not go anywhere.

That does not mean every task has to be local. But for anything involving client names, health details or financial figures, keeping it on your own machine is the safest default — and a strong argument when a client asks where their data goes.

What it can actually do

In everyday terms, a local assistant does the same useful jobs as a cloud one: it can turn a long email thread into a short summary, draft a polite reply in your tone, or help you prepare a document. The difference is where the work happens.

  • Draft and summarise: letters, notes and meeting minutes, without leaving your office.
  • Keep your own memory: the assistant's notes and preferences stay on your machine.
  • Connect to the models you already use: it can work with familiar AI services, while you stay in control.

Updates that can't break your setup

One real fear with self-hosted tools is the update that wrecks everything overnight. The latest OpenClaw release tackles exactly that: before every update it makes a full backup of your setup, and if something goes wrong, it rolls back to the last good state automatically. A busy conversation also no longer freezes everything else, and a restart won't lose the work in progress.

In practice, that means one of the classic worries about running your own software — “what if an update breaks it?” — is now handled for you.

Is it right for you?

Honestly: a local assistant needs a little more setup than just opening a website. If you only ever ask general questions, a normal cloud assistant is simpler and perfectly fine. But if you regularly handle client data and want the reassurance that it never leaves your machine, a local gateway like OpenClaw is worth a look.

The goal is not more technology for its own sake. It is the freedom to use AI help while keeping your clients' trust — and their data — exactly where it belongs.
