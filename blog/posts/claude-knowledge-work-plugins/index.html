Knowledge Work Plugins: AI Assistants With a Job Description

AI AgentsSalesData protection

You know the ritual: every new conversation with an AI assistant starts from zero. You explain what your company offers, how you write, what things cost — and by the time the assistant is actually useful, ten minutes are gone. Anthropic, the company behind the assistant Claude, now publishes free “Knowledge Work Plugins”: ready-made role packs that give the assistant this background once, so you stop repeating it in every chat.

What a “plugin” actually is

Nothing mysterious. A plugin is a small folder of text files: instructions about a job, ready-made commands and — if you want — connections to tools you already use. Think of the handbook you would hand a capable temp on their first day: how we talk to customers, where our documents live, how an offer gets written.

Anthropic publishes eleven of these packs, one per role: sales, customer support, marketing, finance, legal, data work, document search and more. They are free, open source and written in plain language — you do not need to program anything to read or adapt them.

Where this helps a small business

The gain is not a smarter AI — it is a better-prepared one. Three everyday examples:

  • Before a client call: one command pulls together the account history and drafts talking points, so you start informed instead of clicking through folders.
  • Support replies: the support pack answers routine emails in your tone and with your actual policies, ready for you to check and send.
  • Your own knowledge: the search pack points the assistant at your documents, so answers come from your offer sheets and contracts rather than general guesses.

Because the background is stored once, it carries over into every new conversation. The assistant simply stops forgetting your business between chats.

The honest limits

These packs are starting points, not finished assistants. They ship generic: the sales pack knows HubSpot, not your modest little customer list; the finance pack assumes the tools of larger companies. The idea is that you open the files and replace the placeholders with your own services, prices and wording — an afternoon of editing, not a one-click miracle.

You also need a paid Claude plan, and a pack only pays off if you genuinely use Claude for that work. And as always with AI: the drafts still need your review before anything reaches a customer.

What a Swiss business should check first

The connections are the sensitive part. A connector that reads your mail, your CRM or your documents sends that data to an outside service — often one based in the United States. Under the revised Swiss Data Protection Act you remain responsible for client data, no matter which tool touched it.

  • Start without connectors: use the instruction part only — your services, your tone, your routines. The data stays on your side.
  • Connect tools one at a time, beginning with the least sensitive, and check what the service does with what it receives.
  • Never connect client files holding health, financial or legal secrets without a written basis.

One more reason to watch the pattern rather than this one product: the underlying “Agent Skills” standard was handed to an independent foundation, and other assistant makers are adopting it. Role packs like these will likely work across different AI tools before long — understanding the idea is worth more than betting on one brand.

Our take

If you already use Claude, the sales and support packs are a low-risk experiment: try them for a week with harmless example data. If you prefer another assistant, the idea travels anyway — write your own one-page role pack, keep it in a file, and paste it into every conversation. Ten minutes saved per chat, on every chat, is real money for a one-person business.
