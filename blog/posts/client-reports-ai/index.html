Client Reports People Actually Read: An AI Routine for Fiduciaries, Doctors and Notaries

DocumentsClientsWorkflow

A fiduciary in Basel spends Friday afternoon turning a year of bookkeeping into a quarterly client report. A doctor in Geneva writes the same referral summary for the fifth time this week. A notary in Winterthur assembles a status update for a client who simply wants to know where things stand. All three face the same quiet problem: the work is done, but turning it into something the client can actually read and act on takes far longer than it should.

Client reports are one of those tasks that quietly eat a small practice's time. They are not hard in any single step — they are heavy because of everything around them: the blank page, the scattered notes, the fear of leaving something out. A repeatable AI routine removes that weight without ever taking the judgement away from you.

Why reports feel heavier than they should

Most reports are slow for reasons that have nothing to do with the actual content:

  • The blank page. Every report starts from nothing, so you rebuild the structure each time.
  • The raw material is scattered. Notes live in a notebook, numbers in a spreadsheet, decisions in an email thread.
  • The fear of missing something. So everything gets included, and the report grows into a document nobody reads fully.

None of these need more talent to solve. They need a routine.

A simple routine in three steps

Step 1 — Gather the raw material (10 minutes)

Open one place for the report and drop in everything relevant: your notes, the key numbers, the open points, the decisions from the last meeting. Do not tidy or summarise yet — that is exactly what the AI will do.

Step 2 — Let the AI draft, you decide

Hand the raw material to the AI with a clear structure. A good prompt does the heavy lifting:

You are helping me write a client report. Below are my notes for [client]. Turn them into a short, clear report in [language]. Structure it like this: (1) a one-sentence summary, (2) what happened this period, (3) what to pay attention to, (4) one clear next step. Tone: factual, plain language, no jargon. Keep it to one page. Notes: [paste your notes].

Step 3 — Review, approve and send

Read the draft once, correct anything that is wrong, and add your personal touch. You stay the author; the AI only prepared the first version.

What makes a report actually get read

Clients do not read reports for pleasure. They read to find out two things: is everything fine, and do I need to do anything. Build for that:

  • One sentence at the top. "Everything is on track" or "One point needs your decision" — the whole message in a line.
  • Numbers with context. Not "Revenue CHF 84,000" but "Revenue CHF 84,000, up 6% on last year".
  • Plain language. If a word needs a glossary, replace it.
  • Exactly one next step. A report that ends with ten action points ends up in a drawer. One clear step gets done.

One prompt, many reports

The same routine works for the fiduciary's quarterly report, the doctor's referral letter, and the notary's status update — only the structure line changes. If a passage still sounds too technical, a second short prompt fixes it:

Rewrite this passage in plain [language]. Short sentences, everyday words, no specialist terms unless you explain them. Keep the meaning and all numbers exactly the same. Passage: [paste].

A note on data and privacy

Client reports contain names, amounts and sometimes health details — so keep them away from public chatbots unless you anonymise first. Replace names with placeholders, describe the case without copying sensitive passages, and use a local or zero-retention service (such as a Swiss or enterprise tier of Claude or ChatGPT) when the report must stay identifiable. Your professional secrecy is not negotiable — the AI setup must respect it.

The outcome

Reports that used to take a full afternoon now take one hour — and the result is shorter, clearer, and more likely to be read. Clients feel looked after, you stop dreading the end of the quarter, and the work you already did finally shows up in a form people can use.
